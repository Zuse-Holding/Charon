On October 2, 2026, Metis Committee met on Robinhood (HOOD). The vote split two against two, and the meeting page labels it a split decision with 50% unity. It's a useful meeting to read closely, because the analysts looked at the same numbers and reached different answers for reasons you can follow.

The full transcript is public at [committee.metisanalytic.com/meetings/115](https://committee.metisanalytic.com/meetings/115). This is a guided read of it, not a recommendation, and HOOD has moved since.

## The numbers everyone saw

Every analyst got the same fact sheet:

- Price $117.59, up 5.79% that day
- P/E of 52 on trailing earnings, 34 on forward estimates, price-to-book of 11.2
- Revenue up 32.3% year over year, with a 44% operating margin and a 23.6% return on equity
- Debt-to-equity of 240%
- Above both the 50-day ($105.49) and 200-day ($94.35) moving averages, at 60% of its 52-week range (high $153.86)
- 27 Wall Street analysts at buy, average target $131.45

Two things were missing: a historical P/E average and a free cash flow figure. Several analysts said so, and both gaps shaped the outcome.

## Where they agreed

The value and growth analysts both called the growth real and profitable. Value, growth and macro all flagged the 240% debt-to-equity, and the value analyst and the risk officer both pointed out that without a cash flow figure there was no way to check how well that debt is covered. Nobody argued the valuation was cheap.

The disagreement was about what to do with those facts.

## The four votes

**Value: WATCH, 55% confidence, suggested 1.5%.** The value analyst gave the business credit for its margins, then pointed out that the average analyst target was only about 12% above the price. At 52 times earnings, the market was already paying for growth to keep beating expectations. It also discounted the 27 buy ratings on purpose, calling them sentiment rather than valuation.

**Growth: BUY, 68%, suggested 4.5%.** The growth analyst read the same margins the other way: 32% growth with a 44% operating margin is growth that pays for itself, which is rare. The gap between the trailing and forward P/E meant earnings were expected to catch up to the price. It also noted Robinhood moving into crypto, margin lending and wealth products as a source of further growth.

**Macro: WATCH, 50%, suggested 1.5%.** The macro analyst saw Robinhood as a bet on retail traders' appetite for risk, which tends to run with rising markets and falling rates. Its argument was about timing: the friendly rate backdrop was already in the price, and the stock was in the middle of an uptrend rather than near its start. With that much debt, it also noted that a tightening in credit would hurt.

**Technical: BUY, 68%, suggested 4%.** The technical analyst pointed to price above the 50-day average and the 50-day above the 200-day, a 5.79% gain on 6.46 million shares traded, and room left before the 52-week high. It named its own risk: a single big up day can be followed by a pullback.

## The risk officer

The risk officer doesn't vote on direction. It counted the split as two buys at 68% against two waits at 55% and 50%, and concluded the position should be moderate. The portfolio was almost entirely cash, so there was no concentration limit to run into, but that also meant HOOD would be the only holding. It suggested 3% with a hard cap of 4.5%, and recommended leaving room to add rather than buying the full amount the day after a big jump.

## The chairman's call

The chairman decided BUY at 3% of the portfolio, with a blended confidence of 61%.

The direction followed the two bulls. The size followed the risk officer, not the growth analyst's 4.5%. The chairman's reasoning gives two reasons: the 4.5% cap was kept as room to add later, and buying right after a 5.79% day argued for a partial position. It named two conditions for adding the remaining 1.5%: a pullback that holds the 50-day average, or an earnings report that confirms the 32% growth rate.

The bears were outvoted on direction, but their concerns about valuation, debt and timing set the size.

## How this meeting will be graded

Each vote is checked against the $117.59 price 30 days after the meeting, around November 1.

- The chairman's BUY and the two analyst BUYs are correct if HOOD is at or above $119.94, which is 2% higher. They are incorrect at or below $115.24, and partial in between.
- The two WATCH calls are correct if HOOD finishes between $115.24 and $119.94. A larger move in either direction makes them partial.
